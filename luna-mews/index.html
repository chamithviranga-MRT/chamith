01 — The House

A mews,
after the moon

A mews is a lane you only find if someone tells you it is there. Ours runs off Ward Place, and opens into a courtyard that has been part gallery, part kitchen and part guest house since the day the gates were unlocked.

Vibrant canvases and charming antiques sit against limewashed walls in salmon pink and olive grey. Most of the seating is outside, under the trees, which is the point — the house is best used slowly. Coffee from eight, lunch through the afternoon, dinner at the end of the week, and four rooms upstairs for anyone who would rather not leave.

03 — The Table

Breakfast
until it isn't

The café opens at eight and runs to five, mostly outdoors, mostly unhurried. From Thursday to Saturday the courtyard is relit and the kitchen serves dinner.

The coffee programme is built with Damn Fine Coffee, who put their machines and their beans behind the counter — which is a long way of saying the flat white is worth the walk down the lane.

The Table
of Seven

Seven stories, seven plates. Built with chef Derrick Kwa as a tribute to seven Sri Lankan women whose work reshaped fashion, mountaineering, human rights, film, art, community and storytelling.

Each course is designed to enact a life rather than describe it — ingredients that mirror the work, techniques that echo the influence. Seats are limited and released by announcement.
